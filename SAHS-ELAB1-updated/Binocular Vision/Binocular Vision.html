Understanding Binocular Vision

Binocular vision is the visual process where both of your eyes work together as a coordinated team. Each eye captures a slightly different angle of the world, and your brain blends these two images into a single, seamless, three-dimensional view with depth perception. Understanding how our eyes align and how the brain combines these views is the key to helping patients with double vision, lazy eyes, or eye turns.

NPC Simulator

Find the Near Point of Convergence. Measures the closest point where both eyes can pull inwards together before breaking into double vision at the nose.

Cover Test Simulator

Diagnostic tool for manifest eye turns (Tropias). Blocks input to one eye to see if the opposite eye jumps to pick up fixation.

Launch

Worth Four Dot Simulator

Sensory fusion test under red-green filters. Diagnoses whether the brain is fusing normally, ignoring one eye (suppression), or seeing double.

Launch

Vergence Simulator

Evaluates motor vergence thresholds. Measures ocular muscle stamina under base-in (divergence) and base-out (convergence) prism strains.

Launch

Fusion Simulator

Traces cortical sensory fusion. Examines how the visual cortex matches and blends slightly disparate right/left eye inputs into a single scene.

Launch

Suppression Simulator

Maps cortical "shut-off" zones. When eyes point in different directions, the brain blocks the turning eye's feed to protect the patient from seeing double.

Launch

Stereoacuity Simulator

Measures high-grade 3D depth perception in seconds of arc. Determines how accurately the visual cortex calculates physical depth and relative spatial distances.
