Extraocular Anatomy

Extraocular muscles govern eye movements. The four rectus muscles (Superior, Inferior, Medial, Lateral) originate posterior to the globe from the Annulus of Zinn at the orbital apex, while the oblique muscles have unique insertion courses.

Visual Pull Axes & Insertion Distances

The 3D model visualizes the 23° and 51° pull angles relative to the visual axis. The 4 recti attach anteriorly and originate at the Annulus of Zinn at the apex.

Medial Rectus (MR) 5.5mm from limbus
Inferior Rectus (IR) 6.5mm / 23° pull
Lateral Rectus (LR) 6.9mm from limbus
Superior Rectus (SR) 7.7mm / 23° pull
THE COMMON TENDINOUS RING: The Annulus of Zinn serves as the common muscular origin for the 4 recti. It encircles the optic canal and the medial portion of the superior orbital fissure.
OBLIQUE MUSCLE ORIGINS: • Superior Oblique: Originates superior and medial to the optic canal on the body of the sphenoid bone.
• Inferior Oblique: Originates from the anteromedial floor of the orbit (maxillary bone).

Cranial Nerve Palsy Tester

Clinical motor systems correspond to the classic anatomical rule of thumb: LR₆SO₄(All Others)₃.

III
CN III (Oculomotor) Agonist Deficit

Supplies: MR, SR, IR, IO

Palsy: "Down & Out" eye deviation, complete ptosis, pupillary dilation.

IV
CN IV (Trochlear) SO Deficit

Supplies: Superior Oblique (SO)

Palsy: Vertical diplopia, eye elevated on adduction, compensatory head tilt.

VI
CN VI (Abducens) LR Deficit

Supplies: Lateral Rectus (LR)

Palsy: Complete failure to abduct the eye, marked Esotropia in primary position.

Clinical Relationships & Laws

Select a clinical relationship below to visualize physiological mechanisms on the 3D model.

1. Agonist & Antagonist (Sherrington's Law)

Reciprocal innervation: Increased innervation to an agonist (OD LR) causes proportional decreased innervation to its direct antagonist (OD MR).

Simulating: OD Abduction
2. Yoke Muscles (Hering's Law)

Equal innervation: Contralateral synergists (e.g., OD LR & OS MR) receive equal and simultaneous innervation during conjugate gaze.

Simulating: Dextroversion
3. Ipsilateral Synergists

Muscles in the same eye that act together to produce a specific movement (e.g., OD Superior Rectus and OD Inferior Oblique both elevate).

Simulating: OD Elevation
4. Donder's & Listing's Laws

Donder's: Torsional position is fixed for any gaze direction.

Listing's: Rotations occur around an axis in Listing's equatorial plane.

Clinical Reference Manual

Department of Optometry, SAHS

Common Tendinous Ring (Annulus of Zinn)

A circular fibrous ring positioned at the orbital apex surrounding the optic canal. It acts as the common muscular origin for all four recti muscles.

Oblique Muscle Origins

• Superior Oblique (SO) originates from the sphenoid bone body superomedial to the optic canal.
• Inferior Oblique (IO) originates on the anterior orbital floor of the maxilla bone, making it unique as it does not originate at the deep orbital apex.

Cranial Nerve Formula

LR₆SO₄(All Others)₃: Lateral Rectus is innervated by Cranial Nerve VI (Abducens). Superior Oblique is innervated by Cranial Nerve IV (Trochlear). All other muscles (SR, IR, MR, IO) are innervated by Cranial Nerve III (Oculomotor).
