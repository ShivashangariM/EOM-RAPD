Ocular Surface Staining

1. Grading of Ocular Surface Staining

A. CLEK Scheme (Collaborative Longitudinal Evaluation of Keratoconus)

Why is it done?

To quantify corneal fluorescein staining and assess severity of ocular surface damage in keratoconus and dry eye syndrome.

Function Assessed

Corneal epithelial barrier integrity & focal micro-abrasion patterns.

Interactive Diagnostic Visual: CLEK 5-Zone Corneal Diagram Click Zones to Grade (0 - 3)

CLEK Score Assessor (0 - 3 per zone):
Central 0 / 3
Superior 0 / 3
Nasal 0 / 3
Inferior 0 / 3
Temporal 0 / 3
Total Score 0 / 15
Methodology Note: In the CLEK study, grading assesses the density of punctate staining across all segments under slit lamp examination with an excitation filter.

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Fluorescein strip, Sterile saline, Slit lamp, Cobalt blue filter
Interpretation Details

Higher cumulative scores correlate directly with advanced corneal micro-trauma. Inferior-dominant staining patterns suggest incomplete lid closure or evaporative stress.

Seminal Research Results

The CLEK Study Investigators showed that localized corneal staining patterns correlate with decreased functional visual acuity and worsening ocular comfort metrics.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Moisten a high-grade sodium fluorescein strip with one drop of sterile saline (shake off excess).
  2. 2 Instill dye lightly into the patient's inferior conjunctival sac.
  3. 3 Instruct the patient to blink naturally 2 to 3 times to distribute dye evenly.
  4. 4 Visualize under high-illumination cobalt blue filter on the slit lamp.
  5. 5 Examine and score the five designated corneal quadrants individually.

Function Assessed

Diffuse corneal and conjunctival tissue micro-damage.

Grade 0: Normal

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Fluorescein, Lissamine Green, Slit lamp
Interpretation & Cut-offs

Grade 0 indicates clear tissue. Progression to Grade V reveals massive, coalesced cell death, often seen in severe keratoconjunctivitis sicca.

Seminal Research Results

Widely utilized across international dry eye consensus guidelines, the Oxford Scheme shows outstanding reproducibility across clinical evaluators.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Instill dye (fluorescein for cornea, lissamine green for conjunctiva).
  2. 2 Wait 1 to 2 minutes to allow dye binding into disrupted cell boundaries.
  3. 3 Scan corneal and conjunctival planes under moderate magnification.
  4. 4 Compare staining densities directly with standard panel templates to determine Grade (0 - V).

2. Tear Break-Up Time (TBUT)

Why is it done?

To measure the stability of the tear film and rule out evaporative dry eye.

Function Assessed

Tear film stability and mucin/lipid interface uniformity.

Seminal Research Results

Mengher et al. and the TFOS DEWS II consensus report note that diminished TBUT serves as a highly sensitive biomarker for identifying lipid deficiency.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Gently apply a small amount of non-preserved sodium fluorescein into the conjunctival sac.
  2. 2 Have the patient blink naturally several times, then look straight ahead and hold eyes open.
  3. 3 Start the timer immediately following the final complete blink.
  4. 4 Observe the tear film using a broad slit-lamp beam under cobalt blue light; stop timer at the exact moment the first dark non-fluorescent spot appears on the cornea.

Seminal Research Results

The TFOS DEWS II diagnostic report classifies the classic Schirmer-I test as a reflex-stimulated measurement of tear production capacity, invaluable for ruling out systemic Sjögren's syndrome.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Fold a standardized sterile strip at the notch mark.
  2. 2 Place folded end over the lower lid margin at the outer third of the lid (avoiding the cornea).

  4. 4 Remove strip and read the length of wetting from the fold notch in millimeters.

4. Tear Osmolarity

Why is it done?

Tear film hyperosmolarity is recognized as a key step in dry eye development.

Function Assessed

Tear film salt concentration and inter-eye dynamic variability.

Interactive Clinical Tool: Tear Osmolarity Clinical Assessor

Moderate Dry Eye

Inter-eye variability exceeding 8 mOsm/L suggests tear film instability.

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used TearLab/OcuSense Osmometer, microcapillary chip
Interpretation Details

Values above 308 mOsm/L, or a difference between eyes of over 8 mOsm/L, are indicative of dry eye disease.

Seminal Research Results

Tomlinson et al. and DEWS II report a sensitivity of 94% and specificity of 84% for tear osmolarity in dry eye diagnosis.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Insert a new disposable microchip into the collection pen.
  2. 2 Place the microchip tip against the lower tear meniscus, taking care not to touch the eyelid or cornea.
  3. 3 Verify fluid collection is complete via the automated indicator beep on the device.
  4. 4 Dock the collection pen into the analyzer base and note the value.

5. Tear Meniscus Radius, Height & Area

Why is it done?

To measure total baseline tear reservoir volume at the eyelid-globe boundary non-invasively.

Function Assessed

Tear lake reserve capacity and aqueous tear meniscus volume.

Interactive Clinical Tool: Tear Meniscus Profile Assessor

Radius of Curvature
0.18 mm

0.015 mm²
Clinical Diagnosis: Marginal Deficiency

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Slit lamp, Video meniscometer, AS-OCT
Standard Cut-offs

A height measurement below **0.20 mm** points to aqueous deficiency.

Seminal Research Results

Mainstone et al. showed that non-invasive meniscus measurements correlate with the Schirmer test without triggering reflex tearing.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Have the patient look straight ahead to establish a stable tear lake level.
  2. 2 Align the measuring slit beam or optical coherence tomography scanner over the lower lid margin.
  3. 3 Capture a cross-sectional image of the tear wedge.
  4. 4 Measure the meniscus height and radius of curvature to determine tear volume.

Tear Stability

7. Tear Stability Analysis System (TSAS)

Why is it done?

Provides objective, computerized monitoring of tear break-up locations and progression without dye instillation.

Function Assessed

Dynamic non-invasive tear film break-up topography.

Interactive Clinical Tool: Projected Placido Disc Analysis

Simulate Tear Film Break-up Profile:
Stable tear profile active: Concentric placido rings appear uniform with no distortion.

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Videokeratoscope, TSAS software
Seminal Research Results

Goto et al. introduced TSAS to reduce the patient variability associated with dye instillation and manual timing.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Have the patient fixate on the target at the center of the videokeratoscope rings.
  2. 2 Project the concentric placido ring array onto the tear film surface.
  3. 3 Begin high-speed software recording immediately following a full blink.
  4. 4 Analyze the captured data to identify and map areas of placido ring distortion.

8. Meibometry

Why is it done?

Provides a quantitative evaluation of total active meibum lipids residing on the lower lid margin reservoir.

Function Assessed

Meibomian gland active lipid secretion output levels.

Interactive Clinical Tool: Spectrophotometric Lipids Tape Analyzer

Clinical Output: Normal Secretion

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Meibometer device, specialized plastic collection tape strips
Seminal Research Results

Quantifiable meibometry serves as a valuable clinical research endpoint for determining tear film evaporation changes.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Apply the collection tape strip gently against the lower lid margin to collect meibum.
  2. 2 Place the tape strip into the meibometer.
  3. 3 Initiate spectrophotometric measurement to quantify the collected lipid volume.

9. Meibography / Meiboscopy

Why is it done?

Provides a high-definition anatomical visualization of meibomian gland structures inside the tarsal plates.

Function Assessed

Meibomian gland structural dropout, truncation, or atrophy levels.

Interactive Diagnostic Visual: Meibomian Gland Dropout Assessor Drag Slider to Adjust Atrophy

Meiboscale Grade
Grade 0
Severity Description
Healthy parallel gland structure.

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Infrared Meibographer, Slit lamp Meiboscope attachment
Seminal Research Results

Widespread clinical evidence demonstrates a direct correlation between gland dropout percentage and evaporative tear film instability.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Gently evert the lower or upper eyelid to expose the inner tarsal mucosa.
  2. 2 Align the infrared imaging system to capture the meibomian gland anatomy.
  3. 3 Record scans and grade gland dropout from Grade 0 (no loss) to Grade 3 (severe loss).

10. Brush Cytology Technique

Why is it done?

To collect conjunctival epithelial cells for histological or immunological analysis.

Function Assessed

Goblet cell density and squamous metaplasia changes.

Interactive Clinical Tool: Cytological Smear Microscope Simulator

Normal density of mucin-secreting goblet cells present.

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Cytology brush, microscopic slide, fixative, stain
Seminal Research Results

A reliable method for evaluating changes in goblet cell density and surface inflammation in dry eye diseases.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Apply a single drop of topical anesthetic into the eye.
  2. 2 Gently rotate the cytological brush across the conjunctival surface.
  3. 3 Transfer cells onto a glass slide, fix, and prepare for histological staining.

11. Flow Cytometry in Impression Cytology

Why is it done?

To analyze and quantify inflammatory cell markers collected from the ocular surface.

Function Assessed

Cellular immune activity and inflammatory marker levels (HLA-DR).

Interactive Clinical Tool: Flow Cytometry Scatter Analyzer

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Cellulose acetate filter, Flow cytometer
Seminal Research Results

Pisella et al. demonstrated that tracking HLA-DR expression levels using flow cytometry helps quantify inflammatory changes on the ocular surface.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Collect conjunctival cell samples using cellulose acetate filter paper.
  2. 2 Suspend cells in assay buffer and label with fluorescent antibodies targeting HLA-DR.
  3. 3 Run sample through flow cytometer to measure antibody expression levels.

13. Ocular Protection Index (OPI)

Why is it done?

Calculates the relationship between tear stability (TBUT) and blink interval to determine corneal exposure risk.

Function Assessed

Tear film protection and corneal exposure risk profile.

Interactive Clinical Tool: OPI Standard Calculator

sec
sec
Calculated OPI Ratio
0.8
Formula: TBUT / IBI
Exposure Risk Level
OPI < 1 (Unprotected)

The tear film breaks down before the next blink, leaving corneal tissues exposed to dry spot damage.

Parameters & Instruments

Index Calculations
OPI = \frac{\text{TBUT (sec)}}{\text{Interblink Interval (sec)}}
Seminal Research Results

Ousler et al. proved that an OPI < 1 correlates with increased corneal staining and discomfort.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Measure the patient's average TBUT value using standard fluorescein procedures.
  2. 2 Observe and count blinks over a 1-minute interval during a relaxed conversation to establish the Interblink Interval (IBI).
  3. 3 Calculate the ratio by dividing TBUT by IBI to determine the Ocular Protection Index.

14. Fluorophotometry (Fluorimetry)

Why is it done?

Provides a quantitative, objective evaluation of tear clearance and tear turnover rates over time.

Function Assessed

Tear turnover rate and dynamic clearance behavior.

Interactive Clinical Tool: Fluorophotometric Clearance Decay Plotter

Clearance Profile: Normal Tear Flow

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Fluorophotometer, fluorescein dye
Seminal Research Results

A slower fluorescence decay rate indicates reduced tear turnover, suggesting impaired tear clearance.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Instill a standardized volume of fluorescein dye into the eye.
  2. 2 Measure baseline fluorescence intensity using the fluorophotometer.
  3. 3 Measure fluorescence decay at regular intervals to compute tear clearance.

Why is it done?

Integrates tear production output and tear clearance rate measurements into a single unified index value.

Function Assessed

Overall tear functional output and dynamic clearance behavior.

Parameters & Instruments

Instruments Used Schirmer strips, Fluorescein
Seminal Research Results

Xu et al. proposed the TFI to provide a more comprehensive indicator of overall tear film dynamics than Schirmer testing alone.

Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

  1. 1 Perform an anesthetized Schirmer test and record the value in millimeters.
