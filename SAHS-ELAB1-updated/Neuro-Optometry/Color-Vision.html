Clinical Training Academy

Clinical Examination Protocol

  1. Illumination Setup: Position the patient under standardized daylight illumination ($\ge 270 \text{ lux}$).
  2. Arrangement: Direct the patient to look through the scrambled caps, select the cap matching closest in color shade to the fixed pilot, and slide it adjacent to it.
  3. Diagnosis: Examine the polar crossover graphs to identify confusion paths.

Diagnostic Evaluation Metrics

Anomalous Trichromacy

One cone photopigment class experiences a partial shift in its absorption peak. Central opponent channel retains general sequence integrity.

Scorecard Sign: Minor Transpositions

Dichromacy (Anopias)

Absolute genetic absence of an entire cone cell class. Wavelength distinction collapses along standard isometric confusion tracks.

Scorecard Sign: Major Parallel Crossovers

Dyschromatopsia Keys

Congenital vs. Acquired Defects

Congenital defects are autosomal or X-linked inherited conditions causing developmental cone anomalies. Acquired defects result from demyelination (optic neuritis), trauma, toxicity, or retinal degeneration.

Clinical References

Trusted Academic Guidelines & Literature

AAO (Color Vision Assessment)

Ishihara pseudoisochromatic plates evaluate the integrity of the red-green parvocellular pathway. The Farnsworth D-15 test is recommended to differentiate protan, deutan, and tritan deficiencies based on confusion axes.

AOA Clinical Standards

Hereditary dyschromatopsia is predominantly X-linked recessive (Protan and Deutan), affecting roughly 8% of males. Tritan defects are autosomal dominant, mapped to Chromosome 7.

Farnsworth-Munsell 100 Hue Test

FM-100 Hue Test uses 85 distinct color cap steps forming a continuous hue cycle. Total Error Score (TES) represents the sum of deviations of adjacent sorted caps, where higher scores signify severe chromatic deficiencies.
